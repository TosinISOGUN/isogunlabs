Performance review season has a predictable shape. The form goes out, it asks for six or
twelve months of accomplishments, and most people open Jira for the first time in that period
with genuine dread — not because the work wasn't good, but because half of it is gone from
memory. The February project. The bug that took a week to track down in April. The thing you
shipped right before a vacation and never thought about again. None of it was forgotten because
it didn't matter. It was forgotten because nobody was keeping a record while it happened.

## The review isn't the hard part

Writing the actual review, once you know what you did, is the easy half — a few sentences per
project, said plainly. The hard half is reconstruction: scrolling months of closed issues,
guessing at dates, trying to remember which quarter a given piece of work even landed in. That
reconstruction is where the real time goes, and it's also where the review quality suffers —
work that doesn't get remembered doesn't get written down, no matter how much impact it had.

This is a timing problem, not a writing problem. By the time the review form shows up, the
best window for capturing March's work accurately was March.

## Why "just remember better" doesn't work

Nobody can fix this by trying harder to remember at the end of the period. The realistic fix is
to stop needing to: keep a running log of what shipped, close to when it shipped, so review
season is a compilation exercise instead of an archaeology one.

This is exactly the gap [Recap](/get-started/) is built to sit in, and it's worth being precise
about what that means rather than overselling it. Recap generates a report from the Jira issues
you completed in the **last 30 days** — that window is fixed, on purpose, and it doesn't reach
back further than that. It is not a one-click button that produces a six-month performance
review from nothing. What it does instead is remove the friction from doing the thing that
actually solves the six-month problem: running it once a month, every month, and keeping what
it gives you.

## What that looks like in practice

1. **Run Recap at the end of each month**, or on whatever cadence you already use for status
   updates. It reads your completed Jira work, groups it by epic, and hands back a short,
   plain-past-tense draft in a few seconds.
2. **Keep the output somewhere durable** — a running doc, a notes folder, wherever you'd keep
   anything else you don't want to lose. Recap itself stores nothing between runs by design, so
   this step is on you, not the app.
3. **When review season arrives, you're compiling, not remembering.** Twelve months becomes
   twelve short paragraphs you already wrote in real time, not one impossible act of recall
   under deadline pressure.

The monthly habit is doing the actual work here. Recap's job is just making that habit cheap
enough to actually keep — a few seconds and one click, instead of the twenty-minute writing
session that makes people skip it three months in a row and lose the thread entirely.

## The part worth doing today

If review season is close, the honest advice is to start now rather than wait for a
"better" starting point — there isn't one. Whatever's still fresh from the last few weeks is
the easiest it will ever be to write down accurately. Every month after this one only gets
harder to reconstruct, not easier.

[Recap](/get-started/) is free for teams of up to 10 people, live on the Atlassian Marketplace,
and takes about as long to run as it took to read this sentence. The best time to start the
habit was six months ago. The second best time is this month's close-out.
