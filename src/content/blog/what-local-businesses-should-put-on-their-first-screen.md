A visitor decides within a few seconds whether your website is worth their time. Whatever is on the first screen, before they scroll, does most of that work.

Most local business websites waste it on a slideshow, a welcome message or a large logo. This guide covers what belongs there instead, for any business, and then for six kinds of business we build for.

## What every first screen needs

**1. Say what you do and where.** "Family dentist in Leeds" beats "Welcome to our practice." A stranger should know what you are, and whether you are near them, without reading further.

**2. One clear next step.** Pick the single thing you most want a visitor to do (call, book, order, enquire) and make it the most obvious button. A second, quieter option is fine. Five equal choices are not.

**3. Proof you are real.** A genuine photograph of your premises or work, and where you have them, a review or a credential. People trust what they can see.

**4. How to reach you.** A phone number that dials when tapped, and your address or area. Hours too, if people need to know whether you are open.

**5. A design that works on a phone.** Check your first screen on a real phone, not just a desktop window. If the button is below the fold on a phone, the page is not doing its job.

## By type of business

**Restaurants.** The first screen should show the room or the food, say what kind of place it is, and offer **Book a table** or **Order** beside **See the menu**. Hours and address belong near the top. Read how we approach [restaurant websites](/services/restaurant-website-design/).

**Clinics.** A calm photograph, the services you treat, and how to book. A new patient is often nervous, so plain language and a visible phone number matter more than clever design. See [clinic websites](/services/clinic-website-design/).

**Realtors.** A search box or a clear choice between buying and selling, your area, and a face. People hire the person, so a real portrait is worth more than a stock house. See [realtor websites](/services/realtor-website-design/).

**Gyms.** A strong photo of your own gym, your offer for new members, and a button for a free trial. The class timetable should be one tap away. See [gym websites](/services/gym-website-design/).

**Beauty brands.** The product or the brand's world in one large image, and a clear way into the shop. The mood does the selling, so the photography has to be good. See [beauty brand websites](/services/beauty-brand-website-design/).

**Startups.** One sentence that says what the product does and who it is for, a picture of the product itself, and one action: join the waitlist, start free or book a demo. See [startup websites](/services/startup-website-design/).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **A slideshow.** Moving images can hide your message and slow the page down.
- **A "welcome" headline.** It tells the visitor nothing they did not already know.
- **Stock photos of people who are not yours.** Visitors can tell, and it costs you trust.
- **Hiding the phone number or booking button** in a menu.
- **Too many choices.** Every extra button makes the main one less likely to be clicked.
- **Text over a busy photo** that is hard to read, especially on a phone.

## A quick test

Show your first screen to someone who has never heard of your business, for five seconds. Then ask them three questions: What does this business do? Where is it? What would you do next? If they cannot answer all three, the first screen needs work.

## The bottom line

The first screen is not the place for everything you want to say. It is the place for what a visitor needs to decide to stay. Get that right, and the rest of the site has a chance to do its job.
